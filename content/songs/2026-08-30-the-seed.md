devotional: >-
  **Devotional**


  Sometimes the most important work is happening where we cannot see it.


  A seed beneath the soil can look like nothing is happening at all. Yet in the
  darkness, its coat opens, its root reaches down, and new life begins finding
  its way toward the light. Digging it up to check its progress may only disturb
  the very work we are waiting to see.


  *The Seed* grew in much the same way. Even when the song seemed complete,
  something was still developing beneath its words. One small change revealed a
  connection that had been there all along: the work was beyond my reach... I
  need to just reach for the Light.


  And perhaps that is the lesson.


  We are not asked to reach for what God is doing beyond our reach. We are asked
  to trust Him with what is happening in the dark and reach for His Light.


  That movement suggests a kind of faith-tropism: a devotional metaphor for the
  soul orienting itself toward God as a young shoot turns toward light. We may
  not comprehend everything that is happening beneath the surface, but we can
  still choose the direction of our reaching.


  Sometimes what looks like nothing is where the deepest work—and the beginning
  of new life—is already underway, even in this song. It grew underneath until I
  finally found the right word that unlocked a deeper meaning for me.


  A seed does not understand the sun. A root does not understand gravity. Yet a
  living plant responds to what it cannot see or comprehend. It orients itself.
  It grows in the direction it was created to grow. Perhaps faith is something
  like that. I do not have to comprehend the Light in order to turn toward it.


  ## What This Means for You


  Perhaps all of this is true for you as well. You plant words of encouragement.
  You offer kindness. You teach children. You pray for people. You serve where
  God has placed you. You do the work that has been put within your reach, often
  without ever seeing what grows from it.


  Some things belong to your hands. Others belong to God. Faith means learning
  the difference.


  You and I have been given something remarkable: not merely the opportunity to
  do what we have been called to do, but also to feel the joy of living what we
  believe.


  You don't know why, or how it came to be you. You don't know what will come of
  your ministry. You don't know why you were gifted in the ways you were, for
  you truly have been given perfect gifts.


  All of those questions belong with the other things beyond our reach. But we
  know what is within our reach.


  The seeds. You. All that is left, within your reach to do, is to exercise your
  gifts.


  Offer kindness. Pray for those you are burdened for. Teach the children. Sing
  in the choir. Bring meals to the hungry. Minister to the sick.


  Bloom where you are planted. Reach for the Light. And let the rest go.


  God does not ask us to see beneath the soil. He asks us to trust that He can.
  As seeds, He grows us as He has fashioned us to be. And the more we use our
  gifts, and grow in our relationship with the Lord, the greater the joy of
  serving Him.


  We have been given a gift. And perhaps what makes it one of the greatest gifts
  is simply this:


  **You are here. You get to see it grow.**
background: >-
  # Science Notes


  **Imbibition —** Germination begins when a dry seed absorbs water. The seed
  swells, its internal processes reactivate, and the seed coat eventually gives
  way as growth begins.


  **Unseen germination —** Considerable biological activity occurs before
  anything appears above the soil. Enzymes become active, stored nutrients are
  mobilized, cells grow, and the embryo resumes development.


  **The radicle —** The radicle is the embryonic root and is generally the first
  structure to emerge from a germinating seed.


  **Gravitropism —** Roots sense gravity and normally grow downward, while
  shoots generally orient upward. Specialized gravity-sensing cells help the
  young plant establish direction even underground.


  **Seed-coat rupture —** As the embryo grows, the enclosing seed coat must
  yield and split. The seed cannot remain completely closed and unchanged while
  becoming a growing plant.


  **Proper moisture —** Seeds need water to germinate, but more water is not
  necessarily better. Excessively wet soil can limit oxygen and promote decay.


  **Oxygen —** A germinating seed is alive and respiring. It needs oxygen to
  release energy from its stored food reserves and support growth.


  **Disturbance —** Digging up a germinating seed can disturb its environment or
  damage its extremely delicate emerging root and shoot. Observation can
  interfere with the very process being observed.


  **Phototropism and light —** After emergence, light becomes important to
  seedling development. Many shoots respond directionally to light, helping the
  developing plant position its leaves for photosynthesis.


  **Timing —** Germination cannot simply be forced by impatience. Its timing
  depends on the species and conditions such as moisture, oxygen, temperature,
  and dormancy.


  **Faith-tropism (devotional metaphor) —** Not a scientific plant term, but a
  spiritual image drawn from tropism: as a plant orients its growth in response
  to a stimulus, faith can be pictured as the soul orienting itself toward God
  and His Light even when the unseen work is beyond our reach.
